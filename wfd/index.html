What we're trying to do

Winter Field Day is mainly about practicing emergency-style operating in cold, adverse conditions. The contest part is real, but it comes second.

Be ready

Set up and run stations away from home, using alternate power where we can, and practice working through a long, cold night.

Earn objectives

Pick up as many WFDA objectives as we can, including Winlink, a bulletin, a cross-band repeater, and SSTV.

Get everyone on the air

Especially newer operators. Learn from each other, have fun, stay warm, and share good food. A friendly rivalry is welcome, but people come before points.

Rules to know

WSJT-X modes don't count. FT8, FT4, and the rest of that family are not allowed in Winter Field Day, and contacts made on them would be disqualified. Digital contacts have to use other modes such as PSK31, RTTY, or Olivia.

Off-site VHF/UHF contact

Under the contest rules, the person acting as our off-site contact for the VHF/UHF modes cannot be at the field day site at all during the event. We will visit beforehand and set up all the equipment they need. If you want the job, say so on the sign-up form.

Guest operators

Operators without a license class that allows a band can still operate under our call with a control operator. Ask when you sign up.

How we're planning the site

Many stations in a small area interfere with each other, so we are planning antenna placement and band/mode assignments in advance.

Tell us what you're bringing

On the sign-up form, list any antennas, masts, and supports, with lengths, heights, weight, and how long they take to set up. Tell us about radios, filters, RFI gear, and long coax runs (200–300 ft).

Please don't show up unannounced with a radio

An unplanned station could interfere with the ones we have already laid out. If you arrive without telling us, we may ask you to take an operating shift on an existing station. If you tell us ahead of time, we can plan a place for you.

What to bring

Logging

We log with N3FJP on Windows. Bring a laptop that can run it. If you have never used it, learn it before Field Day. We will give general help and look into a training class, but during the event there will be no time to teach.

Headphones

The space is small, so every position needs headphones or earbuds. Bring closed-back headphones or in-ear earbuds. No open-back headphones: they have vented ear cups that let sound leak out, and in a crowded room that audio bleeds onto other stations. A headphone splitter lets a logging partner listen along.

SSB gear

  • Footswitch
  • Boom mic
  • Mic stand

Mark everything with your callsign so it goes home with you.

Food

Tell us which meals you'll eat with us and any dietary needs. We may do a potluck or family-style dinner, or order food and ask everyone to chip in a few dollars. Both options are on the sign-up form.

Overnight

We can't plan on sleeping space inside the cabin. If you need to rest, be ready to sleep safely in your vehicle or another setup. It will be cold, so plan for that. Never run a vehicle engine or generator in an enclosed space while sleeping.

Questions

I've never done Field Day. Can I still come?

Yes. Tell us on the sign-up form, and we will pair you with someone. We want newer operators on the air.

I'm not comfortable on SSB. How can I help?

You can log for someone working SSB, operate CW or an accepted digital mode, or take an overnight shift. Tell us what you are comfortable with and we will fit you in.

What if I only want to work one shift?

That's fine. The form asks how much you want to operate, from as much as possible to a single overnight shift.

Who do I contact?

Justin Rogers, N9HO, Winter Field Day coordinator for CCARA. Contact: jus.k.rog@gmail.com

Is there a schedule or station map?

Not yet. We will post the antenna plan, shift schedule, and meal plan here once the sign-ups are in.
